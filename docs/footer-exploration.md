# DoTheThing Footer Explorations

> **Status: approval-only.** These directions do not modify the live shared footer, the homepage, the FAQ page, or any structured data. They are three ways of presenting the same truthful public identity and support details with less visible copy density.

## Shared rules for every option

Each direction retains the exact landing footer foundation: a single warm-white `#fffefb` panel in the 1240px rail, 1px `#a8afc2` outline, 14px radius, low-contrast offset shadow, Inter text, VT323 only for compact group labels, and responsive stacking. The footer remains a **single shared component** used by every public route; no page-specific variant will be introduced.

The Organization structured data remains the canonical machine-readable source for the full business name, address, phone number, online-only status, owner relationship, and contact details. A visible footer should give a reader a useful support route; it does not need to repeat every field on every page.

| Option | Visible identity density | Address and phone treatment | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 · Minimal Trust Strip** | Lowest | Full street address and phone move to Contact; footer retains support email and the truthful ownership relationship. | The cleanest close for a product-led landing page. |
| **02 · Compact Identity Row** | Medium | Full address, phone, and email live in one quiet identity row beneath the directory. | A visible NAP signal without a dense left-column block. |
| **03 · Address Ledger** | Highest, but ordered | Full ownership, street address, phone, email, and review label live together in a measured two-column ledger. | When a detailed business contact block is important to visitors. |

## Option 01 · Minimal Trust Strip

The brand column keeps only the promise, one plain support sentence, and the **support@dothething.tech** link. A restrained ownership line says: “DoTheThing is an online product by Boundless One Ventures.” Full street address and telephone remain available on the Contact page and in Organization schema, but are not repeated on every page. The review label stays in the lower legal line beside copyright and social links.

**Use when:** the primary aim is a calm landing-page close with the least visible compliance copy. **Do not use when:** the footer itself must serve as a detailed contact block.

## Option 02 · Compact Identity Row

The brand and directory retain their landing layout. The detailed identity material moves out of the brand column into one full-width line after the directory: “Boundless One Ventures · 15B-8-4, Mont Kiara Pines, Jalan Kiara 1, Mont Kiara, 50480 Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia · +60166467024 · support@dothething.tech.” The freshness label becomes a small adjacent stamp rather than a third paragraph.

**Use when:** the site needs a visible, consistent public identity without turning the brand column into a dense biography. **Do not use when:** mobile wrapping of the full street address would feel too detailed for the footer.

## Option 03 · Address Ledger

The brand promise stays short. Next to it, a deliberate “Business details” ledger gives owner, full address, phone, and support email equal hierarchy in two clean labeled rows. The directory stays on the right; a lower rail carries the product-review label, copyright, and social links. This preserves every current visible detail but makes it easier to scan.

**Use when:** a full visible NAP block is genuinely desired. **Do not use when:** the product landing page should feel minimal and the Contact page already carries the detailed business information.

## Selection request

Select **01**, **02**, or **03** after reviewing the visual exploration. Once selected, I will apply that one shared footer design consistently across all pages, retain the established landing-system geometry, and leave machine-readable identity data truthful and complete.

## Visual review

The three explorations were reviewed at desktop width. Each preserves the established single-panel footer geometry, directory-grid alignment, warm-white surface, blue-gray outline, low-contrast offset shadow, Inter hierarchy, and lower legal/social rail. Option 01 is the least dense; Option 02 keeps the full visible NAP material in a distinct low-emphasis row; and Option 03 makes the full contact material easiest to scan, at the cost of the largest visible information block. The live shared footer remains unchanged pending selection.
