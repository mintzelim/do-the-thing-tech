# Dr. Amen Seven Types — Result Content Research

## Source distinction

Amen Clinics presents a proprietary seven-type **ADD/ADHD framework**: Classic, Inattentive, Overfocused, Temporal Lobe, Limbic, Ring of Fire, and Anxious.[^amen] This is the framework that the existing quiz labels and question-weighting are intended to reference.

This must not be presented as the formal clinical taxonomy. The CDC describes three ADHD presentations—predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined—and states that there is no single diagnostic test; diagnosis is a multi-step clinical process.[^cdc]

## Content guardrails for the public quiz

The public result should say that the response pattern **“leans toward”** or **“matches the quiz’s [type] pattern”** rather than saying a user *has* a type. Each type result can contain a warm, non-clinical reflection in three parts: **“What can feel tricky,” “A strength to lean on,” and “A kind next step.”** The strength language should be presented as a possibility rather than a universal claim, and the next step should be a low-risk productivity action rather than health treatment.

The existing non-diagnostic boundary should remain prominent and should distinguish this result from an ADHD diagnosis or a formal clinical presentation. The result should not imply that SPECT imaging, this questionnaire, or the seven labels diagnose ADHD.

## Drafting map for the public result

The source framework describes broad symptom themes. The public quiz should translate these into everyday, non-clinical wording and avoid any treatment recommendation, scan claim, or universal personality claim.

| Quiz type | Source-theme summary | Safe public-result translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classic | Distractibility, disorganization, impulsivity, restlessness, and follow-through difficulty. | Fast starts and plenty of momentum can make finishing feel slippery. |
| Inattentive | Distractibility, daydreaming, time-management and follow-through difficulty. | Important things can drift out of view until something really catches your attention. |
| Overfocused | Difficulty shifting attention, worry loops, inflexible or stuck thinking. | Once a thought or plan hooks you, switching gears can feel surprisingly hard. |
| Temporal Lobe | Memory, mood, and reactivity themes in the source framework. | Busy moments can feel more intense, with details or feelings landing loudly. |
| Limbic | Low energy, negativity, low mood, guilt, and reduced interest themes in the source framework. | On low-gear days, the emotional weight of a task can make its first step feel larger. |
| Ring of Fire | Overwhelm, racing thoughts, sensitivity, irritability, and intense input themes. | When everything arrives at once, your brain can feel like it has sixteen tabs playing at full volume. |
| Anxious | Worry, tension, predicting the worst, freezing, and fear of judgment. | “What if?” can grow from a useful check into a full-screen warning. |

The proposed result cards pair each theme with a non-clinical, possibility-based strength and a low-risk productivity next step. This maintains the requested cute, empowering tone while avoiding an unsupported promise that a seven-type quiz establishes a user’s diagnosis or treatment needs.

[^cdc]: [CDC, “Symptoms of ADHD”](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/signs-symptoms/index.html), reviewed 30 July 2026; accessed 18 August 2026.
