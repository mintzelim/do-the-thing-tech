# Blog Source Verification — Working Record

**Status:** Full-corpus source-verification audit in progress, 24 August 2026.

The deterministic verifier inspected **187 unique external URLs** referenced by the 31 canonical blog Markdown files. Its complete machine-readable record is [`blog-source-verification.json`](./blog-source-verification.json). Each entry preserves the original citation URL, every article that references it, final redirected URL, HTTP status, retrieved page title where available, and a reachability classification.

## Immediate findings to remediate

The first pass identified multiple demonstrably broken destinations, including retired CHADD article URLs, retired Understood.org article URLs, an obsolete Child Mind Institute page, several invalid DOI destinations, an unavailable Health Psychology Research PDF, and stale ADDitude links. These must be replaced with current primary or authoritative sources, or removed where no like-for-like source can support the attached claim.

Several commercial publisher pages returned access restrictions rather than a confirmed 404. Those require manual source-identity review or replacement with an accessible primary source; a 403 must not be treated as evidence that a citation is accurate. The remediation must also review title/source identity and claim scope, not merely HTTP status, because a reachable page can still be mismatched or insufficient for a medical, prevalence, treatment, or product claim.

No source will be replaced with an invented citation. Every correction will be validated by a fresh HTTP check and recorded in the final audit report before publication.

## Targeted identity checks — 24 August 2026

Independent page extraction confirmed that the following CHADD pages are live and accurately identify their intended subject despite the sandbox verifier’s fetch errors: [CHADD home](https://chadd.org/), [Executive Function Skills](https://chadd.org/about-adhd/executive-function-skills/), and [General Prevalence of ADHD](https://chadd.org/about-adhd/general-prevalence/). They will be retained and marked as manually verified rather than removed.

The same check identified clear source-identity failures that must be removed: [DOI 10.1038/mp.2009.43](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2009.43) resolves to a bipolar-disorder genetics paper, not an ADHD/dopamine source; [PubMed 27482889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27482889/) is a diabetic-kidney-disease paper, not adult ADHD prevalence; and [PubMed 27866128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27866128/) is a cardiology review, not an adult-ADHD review. [PMC9187895](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9187895/) is a 2022 study titled *Do Executive Dysfunction, Delay Aversion, and Time Perception Deficit Predict ADHD Symptoms and Early Academic Performance in Preschoolers*; any label must reflect that narrower population and topic. [PMC5884954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5884954/) is Rubia’s 2018 review, *Cognitive Neuroscience of ADHD and Its Clinical Translation*, not a Bunford et al. emotion-regulation study.

For this publication pass, the site will also exclude otherwise plausible citations that were inaccessible to independent retrieval because they returned publisher bot-wall responses. This is deliberately conservative: a bot-wall result does not establish that an article is false, but it does not meet the project’s verification bar. The final visible source set will therefore contain only destinations that are directly reachable or specifically manually verified above.

Official provider pages were also checked before being used to restore factual source coverage in the older tools guides. [Microsoft To Do](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/microsoft-to-do-list-app) confirms My Day, reminders, cross-device access, free availability, and task steps. [Todoist Features](https://www.todoist.com/features) confirms Quick Add, natural-language dates, projects, priorities, and sub-tasks. [TickTick](https://ticktick.com/) confirms task organisation, calendar views, Pomodoro, habits, reminders, and cross-platform syncing. These pages support factual product-feature descriptions only; they do not support claims that a product is clinically ADHD-specific or superior for every reader.

The final fetch-error review found that [DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00541](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00541) is *Gender Disparity in the Risk of Hypertension in Subjects With Major Depressive Disorder*, not an ADHD/neuroscience source. It has been removed from article 01. The [Guilford Press record](https://www.guilford.com/books/Attention-Deficit-Hyperactivity-Disorder/Russell-Barkley/9781462517725) is a live publisher page for *Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment*, Fourth Edition, edited by Russell A. Barkley, and is retained with manual verification.

The remaining fetch-error identity checks removed two more mismatches: [DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00584](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00584) is an editorial on psychosis and personality disorders, not an ADHD/dopamine source; and [Springer DOI 10.1007/s10802-018-0470-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10802-018-0470-2) is *Sensitivity to Peer Feedback in Young Adolescents with Symptoms of ADHD*, authored by Babinski et al., not the declared Bondü and Richter citation. Both have been removed. Conversely, [DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2021.12.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2021.12.004) correctly resolves to the 2022 meta-analysis *Altered Perceptual Timing Abilities in ADHD* and is retained with manual verification.

The final women-and-ADHD checks confirm two source identities that the batch verifier could not retrieve consistently. [PMC12145478](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145478/) is Osianlis et al.’s 2025 *ADHD and Sex Hormones in Females: A Systematic Review*; its conclusions are explicitly limited by 11 heterogeneous studies and are therefore represented in the article with qualified language. [Understood.org’s 17 June 2024 release](https://www.understood.org/en/press-releases/new-understood-org-research-finds-adhd-in-women-is-overwhelmingly) is a Harris Poll-backed awareness survey, not clinical prevalence evidence; its methodology and scope are retained in the verification ledger so it is not used to support broader medical claims.

The last intermittent failures also produced one removal and one retention. [PMC7907627](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7907627/) is *Mechanobiological Principles Influence the Immune Response in Regeneration: Implications for Bone Healing*, not an ADHD medication or cognition paper, and has been removed from article 06. [AIMS Public Health DOI 10.3934/publichealth.2024015](https://www.aimspress.com/article/doi/10.3934/publichealth.2024015) is Turjeman-Levi et al.’s 2024 study, *Executive function deficits mediate the relationship between employees' ADHD and job burnout*, and has been manually verified and retained.

## Completed verification baseline

The final deterministic run on **24 August 2026** covers all **31** canonical articles, **137** active source references, and **70** unique external destinations. It reports **57 directly reachable** destinations and **13 independently manually verified** destinations, with **zero** broken, soft-404, access-restricted, fetch-error, or unresolved title-mismatch items in the generated worklists. Confirmed incorrect, inaccessible, or mislabelled sources were removed; sources were never replaced with invented citations. This verifies destination availability and source identity, not a guarantee that every sentence in a cited publication supports every possible interpretation.
