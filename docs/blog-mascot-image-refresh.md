# Blog Mascot Image Refresh

## Purpose

Every blog illustration will use the established DoTheThing mascot rather than human characters, alternate creature designs, or unrelated visual systems. The refresh replaces all 31 featured images and the 21 existing inline editorial images in posts 25–28.

## Canonical Mascot Contract

The subject is a compact upright lavender rounded-rectangle creature rendered in crisp 8-bit pixel art. It has a lavender front face, deep navy-purple side/shadow planes, a dark navy pixel outline, a small upper-left highlight, two small evenly spaced navy square eyes, and small block feet and arms when visible. Its expression is minimal and friendly. It must not have a mouth, hair, human anatomy, clothing, animal features, gradients, rounded-vector rendering, or a second competing character identity.

## Shared Image Direction

Featured images use a 3:2 editorial landscape composition with the mascot as the focal point and a generous warm-off-white #fffefb background. Article-specific props should communicate the subject without on-image words, labels, UI screenshots, charts, logos, badges, or dense data panels. Every image uses restrained lavender, deep navy, muted indigo, limited lime, and warm gray accents with a clear 8-bit pixel grid. Inline images use the same system but focus on one visual idea at a time, with no rendered text; the article headings and alt text remain the source of explanatory detail.

## Replacement Scope

| Asset group | Count | Replacement rule |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Article featured images | 31 | One canonical-mascot scene per post, tailored to the article topic without changing its title, copy, metadata, or alt text. |
| Inline editorial images | 21 | Mascot-led visual explanations for the existing image locations in posts 25–28; no human character, generated text, or competing dark game-dashboard style. |

## Acceptance Check

Each replacement must show the canonical mascot clearly, retain crisp pixel-art edges and warm editorial backgrounds, contain no human figures or readable generated text, and fit the article’s existing image slot without changing its surrounding article copy, semantics, or metadata.
