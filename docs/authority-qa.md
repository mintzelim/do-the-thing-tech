## Source notes used in the August 2026 editorial updates

The National Institute of Mental Health explains that ADHD can affect organizing tasks, managing time, meeting deadlines, and completing work requiring sustained attention. It also identifies breaking large tasks into more manageable steps as a practical coping strategy. Source: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know

CHADD’s workplace guidance lists practical strategies that include reducing distractions, using written reminders, completing one task at a time, breaking long projects into shorter milestones, and obtaining constructive feedback. It also emphasizes that workplace strategies should reflect an individual’s specific challenges. Source: https://chadd.org/for-adults/workplace-issues/

The CDC notes that some adults with ADHD may find workplace accommodations helpful for staying on task or limiting distractions, while needs and supports vary by person. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/about/adhd-in-adults.html
