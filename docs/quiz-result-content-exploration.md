# ADHD Type Quiz Result Content Exploration

This approval-only content exploration keeps the selected Gentle Questline visual direction and the direct **ADHD Type Quiz** framing. It proposes a warmer result structure for every type: **The tricky bit**, **Your bright side**, and **A kind next step**.

The result header remains direct—such as **“Your quiz type: Anxious ADHD”**—but the body uses the truthful boundary established in the research note: the type is what the quiz response *leans toward*, not a diagnosis or clinical presentation.[^research]

| Type | The tricky bit | Your bright side | A kind next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classic ADHD | Momentum can sprint ahead of the plan, leaving the finish line behind. | You can bring spark, courage, and momentum to a stuck room. | Pick one tiny **done** line before you begin. |
| Inattentive ADHD | Important things can drift out of view until they become urgent. | When something clicks, you can bring unusually deep thought to it. | Put the next action where your eyes will actually meet it. |
| Overfocused ADHD | Switching gears can feel like asking a train to turn on a coin. | You can stay with a meaningful idea long enough to notice the good bits. | Set one gentle “switch tracks” cue before you dive in. |
| Temporal Lobe ADHD | A busy moment can land loudly and throw your internal rhythm off. | You may notice nuance, memory cues, and emotional detail that others miss. | Shrink the next action until it feels safe to start. |
| Limbic ADHD | On a low-gear day, the emotional weight of a task can make it look enormous. | You can bring care and meaning to the things that matter to you. | Choose the smallest possible version of today’s task. |
| Ring of Fire ADHD | It can feel like sixteen tabs are all playing at full volume. | Your mind can generate energy and ideas at a remarkable pace. | Turn down one input, then choose one task—not all sixteen. |
| Anxious ADHD | “What if?” can turn from a useful check into a full-screen warning. | You think ahead, notice risks, and care about doing right by people. | Give yourself a two-minute first step—no perfect plan required. |

## Recommended visible result sequence

1. **Your quiz type: [type]** with the existing quiz-first title.
2. A small line: “Your answers leaned toward this pattern in the quiz.”
3. A three-part `result reflection` panel with the wording above.
4. The existing concise non-diagnostic note, stating that the quiz is not a clinical assessment and that a qualified clinician can help with formal evaluation.
5. A **Try the task tool** action, using the chosen next step as an optional cue.

[^research]: See `docs/amen-seven-types-result-content-research.md`; source links include Amen Clinics’ framework description and the CDC clinical-presentation/diagnosis guidance.
